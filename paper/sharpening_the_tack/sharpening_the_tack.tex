\begin{abstract}
Biological aging is classically understood through molecular degradation, yet its macroscopic dynamic signatures remain poorly quantified. This study addresses a fundamental core question: can we mathematically detect and computationally reverse biological aging utilizing continuous-time State Space Models? We frame systemic senescence through a thermodynamic lens, operating under the hypothesis that aging manifests as a measurable geometric flattening of the macroscopic Waddington basin. This topological decay compromises the stability of homeostatic attractors. Consequently, we further hypothesize that computationally injecting exogenous precision into these state spaces will artificially steepen the basin geometry, thereby executing a thermodynamic intervention to rescue failing biological limit cycles. By formalizing the epigenetic landscape mathematically, this work provides a rigorous computational framework to track the geometric deterioration associated with aging and demonstrates how targeted precision modulation acts as an algorithmic intervention to restore robust systemic periodicities.
\end{abstract}
