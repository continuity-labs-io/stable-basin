Loss of dynamical resilience, meaning slower recovery from perturbation, has been proposed as a signature of biological aging. It is often described as a flattening of a Waddington-style potential. We present Stable Basin, an open-source framework that learns a smooth energy function with state-dependent precision from multivariate physiological time series. We ask whether the curvature of that learned landscape, alongside observable early-warning indicators, can detect such a loss. Because a model's curvature is not directly observable, we first test the framework against known ground truth. We use posture time series from held-out C. elegans in the EigenWorms dataset, which contains wild-type and mutant strains but no age information. We establish a worm-level null by comparing disjoint, strain-stratified groups of unperturbed animals, then inject a synthetic degradation that slows gait-amplitude relaxation by known factors. Under the null, the false-positive rate was 0.045. The smallest injected slowdown detected with 80% power was not reached (max power 33.0% at s=3.0), with effect size +0.256 [-0.05, +0.55]. Curvature did not track the injected slowdown. Finally, we model a candidate intervention as an inverse-temperature scaling of the learned dynamics. We score it by distributional distance to a healthy reference in observable space, not by model curvature. The synthetic degradation serves as a positive control for detection and does not claim to reproduce biological aging.
